21DEGREES Blog Review

The blog has a strong, recognisable voice and covers trending topics quickly. A few technical gaps are holding it back on Google and in AI answers. This page covers what we found, what to change, and the system we built to write new posts from Google Trends.

What is already working

  • A voice people remember. Short, punchy openings such as Two brothers from Bihar. Zero inheritance. One year. $1.5 billion. Second person, confident, with an edge.
  • Speed on the news. Posts land while a topic is still trending: an RBI rate move, a Supreme Court GST ruling, an India vs Zimbabwe match.
  • Real Indian detail. Rupee figures, GST sections, ICAI fee scales and named regulators make the posts credible to Indian founders.
  • A consistent brand identity. Posts repeatedly tie "21DEGREES" to "Virtual CFO" and "India". The firm credits this for a client who found it through ChatGPT.
  • One post that already fits the target model. The tax accountant fees guide answers a clear search question with tables and price ranges. New posts should follow its lead.

Every recent post follows the same order

The structure is borrowed from direct-response sales copy. Steps 4 to 9 are close to word-for-word identical across posts, roughly 300–500 words each time. Google treats repeated blocks like this as duplicate content.

  1. Punchy opening: short fragments or a shock statistic
  2. The numbers: The Numbers That Should Terrify You
  3. Three to six numbered problems or lessons
  4. Enter 21DEGREES Advisory Services Private Limited…
  5. We Are Not Just Another Virtual CFO: Pioneers, Not Imitators · Partners, Not Vendors
  6. The Bottom Line (10 of 15) or The Final Verdict (5 of 15)
  7. Are You Ready to Stop Being a…?
  8. P.S. – Still not sure? Let me leave you with this…
  9. The question is not whether you can afford us…

What is holding rankings back

Ordered by likely impact. Every item was measured from the live site.

ImpactIssueWhat we found
HighEvery post has the same meta descriptionAll 144 posts use "Virtual CFO services in Delhi, Noida & Goa". The SEO plugin default was never changed, so Google writes its own snippets and click-through drops.
HighPosts never link to each otherNo post links to another post. Search engines and AI tools can't see the depth of expertise, and service pages get no boost.
HighStatistics have no sources14 of the 15 recent posts quote figures (for example "41% of restaurants never make a profit") without a link. AI assistants rarely repeat claims they can't verify.
HighNo question-and-answer contentNo FAQ sections and no FAQ schema. Headings are slogans ("The Bottom Line") rather than the questions people search. This rules out Google's "People also ask" and AI Overviews.
MediumTitles are too long and lead with the newsTitles run 41–125 characters. News sites win the news term, and the finance keyword sits at the end.
MediumThin author profileEvery post has the same byline, with no bio, credentials or LinkedIn link. Google looks closely at author expertise on finance topics.
MediumDuplicate postsTwo "Why Traditional Virtual CFO Models…" posts, two tax-fee guides, two identical e-commerce posts and two GST rate-change posts compete with each other.
MediumCategories carry no meaningAll 144 posts are tagged both Finance and Virtual CFO. RBI and restaurant posts are also tagged Career.
LowImages and housekeepingMost posts have no in-article images, and alt text is often empty or reused. One URL starts with /title-. There is no llms.txt file to describe the site to AI tools.

The blueprint for new posts

Keep the voice and the speed. Change the structure so the same post can rank on Google and be quoted by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.

Today The Supreme Court Just Made Your Vendor Your Biggest Liability. 63 chars · no keyword · /the-supreme-court-just-made-your-vendor-your-biggest-liability/
Proposed title tag GST ITC Rule: Supreme Court Makes Buyers Pay for Vendor Default keyword first · /gst-itc-supreme-court-vendor-default/

The punchy line can stay as the headline on the page. The title tag shown in Google leads with the search term.

  1. Headline in the house voice, naming the trending topic
  2. Quick answer of 40–60 words directly under it. This is the part AI assistants quote.
  3. The punchy opening, kept from today's posts
  4. What happened, with at least two linked sources (RBI, CBIC, court orders, major newspapers)
  5. Three to six sections headed by real questions, each opening with a one-line answer
  6. A table or checklist and a worked example with rupee figures for an Indian business
  7. One short 21DEGREES section, under 150 words and written fresh for the topic
  8. FAQ of four to six questions, with FAQ schema for Google
  9. P.S. and booking link, plus three to five links to related 21DEGREES posts

The system we built: from Google Trends to a draft post

A small program runs these steps. It writes a draft that is ready for review, and a person approves every post before it goes live.

  1. Read trendsToday's trending searches in India, from Google Trends
  2. Pick topicsClaude scores each trend for search volume, how naturally it leads to a finance lesson, and the keyword to target
  3. ResearchClaude searches the web for facts and figures, and keeps every source link
  4. Write and checkWrites in the house style, then checks against the blueprint. Up to two automatic rewrites.
  5. Review and publishYour team checks the facts, adds an image and publishes on WordPress

How topics are scored

Sport and celebrity stories bring real traffic to this blog, so they stay fully in play. Finance news gets a small boost because it also ranks on finance searches after the news fades.

1.15×Finance and business news: RBI, GST, budget, court rulings, startups
1.0×Sport, celebrity, film, politics and science, used as a business lesson
0×Tragedies, and topics with no sensible business angle

score = search volume (log scale) × fit (0–10) × angle weight

What every draft is checked for

  • Title of 60 characters or fewer, a meta description of 140–160 characters, and a short keyword-led URL
  • 1,400–2,000 words, with at least half of the section headings phrased as questions
  • Every outside link came from the web research, so no invented sources
  • Every link to 21DEGREES points to a page that exists
  • Three to five brand mentions, and none of the repeated stock phrases

One-time fixes for the website

  1. Write a real meta description for each post, starting with the posts that get the most visits.
  2. Merge or redirect the duplicate posts.
  3. Replace the categories with six topic hubs: Virtual CFO, GST and compliance, cash flow, funding and banking, industry playbooks, tax planning. Give each hub a main page that links to its posts.
  4. Add an author page with credentials and a LinkedIn link, and a "reviewed by" line on finance posts.
  5. Add links, an FAQ and sources to the strongest existing guides (fees, when to hire a Virtual CFO, what a Virtual CFO package includes).
  6. Publish an llms.txt file that summarises services, locations and key guides for AI tools.

Method: all 144 posts in the site's post sitemap were crawled on 24 November 2026. "Top 15" means the 15 newest posts, which lead the blog page (23 July – 4 August 2026). Traffic data was not part of this review. Adding Search Console data would show which of these patterns bring the most clicks.
